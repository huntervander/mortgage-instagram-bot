🏠 What is PMI — and do you really have to pay it?

If you're buying a home with less than 20% down on a conventional loan, you've probably heard the term PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance). Here's what you need to know:

What is PMI?
PMI protects the lender — not you — if you stop making payments. It's typically added to your monthly mortgage payment and can range from 0.5%–1.5% of your loan amount annually.

How do you avoid it?
• Put down 20% or more on a conventional loan
• Ask about lender-paid PMI options (may mean a slightly higher rate)
• Use an FHA loan (note: FHA has its own mortgage insurance premium, or MIP)
✅ VA loan borrowers: no PMI, ever — one of the biggest benefits of the VA loan program for eligible veterans and service members

When does PMI go away?
On conventional loans, you can request PMI removal once you reach 20% equity in your home — and it automatically cancels at 22%.
