Should you "buy down" your mortgage rate? Here's what discount points actually mean. 🏡

When you close on a home loan, your lender may give you the option to pay **discount points** upfront in exchange for a lower interest rate over the life of the loan.

Here's the breakdown:
• 1 discount point = 1% of your loan amount paid at closing
• Each point typically reduces your rate by roughly 0.25% (varies by lender and market conditions)
• The lower rate means a smaller monthly payment — but you pay more out of pocket today

The question that matters most: **What's your break-even point?**

Divide the upfront cost of the points by your monthly savings. That tells you how many months it takes to recoup the cost. If you plan to stay in the home longer than that — buying points can make real sense. If you're planning to move or refinance sooner, you might be better off keeping that cash.

Buying points isn't right for everyone, and the math changes based on your loan size, rate environment, and timeline. Let's talk through your specific situation together.
