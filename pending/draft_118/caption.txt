Your credit score is one of the biggest factors in your mortgage rate — here's what every homebuyer needs to know. 🏡

When lenders look at your application, your credit score helps determine both whether you qualify and what interest rate you'll be offered. A higher score typically means a lower rate — and over a 30-year loan, even a 0.5% difference can add up to tens of thousands of dollars.

Here's a general breakdown of how scores tend to impact mortgage eligibility:

• 760+ — Usually qualifies for the best available rates
• 700–759 — Good rates, solid loan options (Conventional, FHA)
• 640–699 — FHA and some Conventional programs still accessible; rates may be higher
• Below 620 — Fewer options, but don't count yourself out — let's talk!

A few tips to strengthen your score before you apply:
✔ Pay down revolving credit card balances
✔ Avoid opening new credit accounts in the 6 months before applying
✔ Dispute any errors on your credit report (it's free at AnnualCreditReport.com)
✔ Don't close old accounts — length of credit history matters

Even if your score isn't where you'd like it to be right now, there are loan programs designed to help — and small improvements can move you into a better rate bracket faster than you'd expect.
