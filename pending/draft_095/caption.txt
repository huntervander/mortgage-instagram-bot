You found a great interest rate — but did you check the APR? 👀

These two numbers look similar on paper but mean very different things, and mixing them up can cost you more than you think.

🔑 Here's the difference:

📌 Interest Rate = the base cost to borrow the money. This is what drives your monthly principal + interest payment.

📌 APR (Annual Percentage Rate) = the interest rate PLUS lender fees rolled into one number — origination fees, points, mortgage insurance, and certain closing costs included. It reflects the true "all-in" cost of the loan.

Why does this matter when you're shopping loans?

A loan with a lower interest rate but higher fees can actually be more expensive over time. APR makes it easier to compare loan offers from different lenders on an even playing field.

💡 Quick rule of thumb:
• If you plan to stay in the home long-term → focus more on the interest rate (lower monthly payment wins over time)
• If you might sell or refinance within 5–7 years → pay closer attention to upfront fees (the APR gap matters more short-term)
